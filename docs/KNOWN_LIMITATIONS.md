# Known limitations

Updated: 2026-08-31.

- The official ReactSeq converter requires a legacy isolated runtime. Reproduction on this Apple Silicon host has not yet been established. Three committed golden cases are copied from pinned official repository demo input/output files, but the local adapter remains labelled a source-inspected safe subset rather than full official compatibility.
- ReactSeq checkpoints are linked externally rather than committed at the pinned source revision. MEO embedding and token-probability support is unavailable until a checkpoint is checksum-pinned and inference is reproduced.
- Official SynthEx ReactionJSON and RouteJSON specifications are absent at the checked commit. Only the visibly named `synthaudit.synthex-paper-draft/0.1` adapter can be implemented; official adapters fail closed.
- Synthelite route exports are implementation artifacts rather than a stable cross-project schema and may change. The current adapter accepts one fixed nested `ReactionTree.to_dict` tree only when each reaction carries explicit mapped reaction SMILES. Route sets, alternative reaction branches, and unmapped nodes are rejected; unknown data is preserved and unsupported cases are reported.
- The deterministic graph engine checks representation and structure, not laboratory outcome, kinetics, selectivity, safety, yield, or scale.
- RDKit sanitation and CIP assignment have representation/version-dependent edge cases, especially aromatic/Kekulé, organometallic, pseudo-asymmetric, atropisomeric, and cyclic stereo chemistry. Diagnostic mode never upgrades these cases to valid.
- Fragment participation in mapped reaction SMILES can be ambiguous when reagents are atom-mapped or atoms are unbalanced. The adapter warns and does not silently map or repair.
- Novelty values depend on corpus composition, preprocessing, fingerprints, and time. Missing reference data returns unavailable evidence, not a score.
- Precedent similarity is not experimental validation. Reported yield/conditions, when legally available, may be noisy or context-dependent.
- Initial evidence models are baselines. Calibration is conditional on declared data and may not transfer out of distribution.
- Route condition compatibility and protecting-group timing begin with transparent rules and review flags; they are not comprehensive synthetic-chemistry reasoning.
- Large MVP/full datasets, paid-provider prompt experiments, GPU models, and laboratory validation are outside offline tests and are not claimed complete without artifacts.
- The Phase 1 CLI contains only bootstrap/version and small-manifest validation commands; reaction commands are implemented after the canonical schemas and execution engine.
- IR schema validation intentionally checks representation shape, edit invariants, roles, IDs, and dependency references; SMILES parsing, atom-map uniqueness, valence, sanitation, and chemistry-aware no-op checks belong to the graph/audit stages and are not implied by successful Pydantic validation.
- Introduced external fragments must already carry the exact next sequential atom maps in ReactionIR. Adapters allocate them; the executor rejects rather than renumbering an ambiguous IR payload.
- Absolute tetrahedral assignment is conservative: duplicate canonical substituent ranks are marked indeterminate. Advanced pseudo-asymmetric and cyclic cases require dedicated audit evidence rather than forced configuration.
- A full transaction may carry an explicitly diagnostic, unsanitized synthon into completion when the sole core-stage failure is sanitation and completion later yields a sanitized graph. The nested core result remains failed and retains the RDKit error. Null ReactSeq completion uses integral lost bond-order capacity to materialize hydrogen with a warning; fractional aromatic, metal, radical, and unusual valence cases remain review items.
- ReactSeq attachment bond order follows the pinned upstream valence rules because the tail string does not encode that bond order directly. The rule and warning are preserved; broader conformance requires more official fixtures.
- The three-case ReactSeq demo fixture has 100% local parse/execution/exact-reconstruction only as a regression observation for those three cases. It is not reported as general ReactSeq accuracy, and it contains no stereo case.
- Cross-representation comparison aligns atom-map renumbering through product-graph isomorphism. If a symmetric product admits correspondences with different edit meanings, the result is `indeterminate`; it is never resolved by arbitrary canonical ranks. “Equivalent except unspecified stereo” describes information loss, not proof that either stereochemical outcome is chemically supported.
- The SynthEx route namespace is the local `synthaudit.synthex-paper-draft-route/0.1` envelope. It is not official RouteJSON, and no conversion to a future official schema is promised.
- Structural audit uses RDKit sanitation plus transparent representation-level heuristics. The edit-count threshold (default 12), absolute formal-charge review threshold (>3), single-heavy-atom warning, and uncommon-fragment element/size rule are review triggers, not empirical reactivity rules.
- Reaction-centre ring consistency currently compares ring-count change with declared add/break topology operations; it does not enumerate mechanistic ring strain or pericyclic pathways. Alternative-site detection uses graph symmetry without forcing a canonical-map choice.
- Completion-stage precursor comparison intentionally removes stereo before comparison because stereo is audited in the next stage. Corpus-based leaving-group novelty remains `unavailable` until Phase 7 supplies a versioned reference index.
- Stereo audit covers RDKit-supported tetrahedral and double-bond E/Z semantics. Pseudo-asymmetric, atropisomeric, enhanced stereo groups, coordination chemistry, and some ring-constrained assignments require expert review and can remain `indeterminate`.
- The Phase 6 HTML report is a stage-specific core report. Novelty, precedent, calibrated evidence, route sections, and the complete release layout are added only when their real providers and schemas exist; absent sections are not populated with placeholders that resemble results.
- Phase 7 novelty is corpus-relative and fingerprint-relative. Morgan and hashed semantic fingerprints can collide and are sensitive to preprocessing, tautomer/protonation representation, reference coverage, and RDKit version. The 0.5 familiarity and 0.7 close-centre thresholds are transparent descriptive defaults, not empirically validated decision boundaries.
- Scaffold novelty is unavailable for acyclic scaffold-free structures. Optional DRFP, ReactSeq MEO, and reaction-taxonomy evidence remains explicitly unavailable unless a versioned provider and its artifact provenance are configured.
- Precedent ranking is lexicographic across six independently visible axes. It is not a learned relevance model, a procedure-transfer guarantee, or experimental validation; sparse stereo, conditions, procedure text, and reported yields remain missing rather than inferred.
- The synthetic two-record Phase 7 smoke index verifies calculation and provenance plumbing only. It is not a chemistry benchmark, and its exact self-match values cannot be generalized to an external corpus.
- The committed Phase 8 benchmark is an authored 200-record software-verification fixture, not a licensed research corpus. Its `recorded_reaction` label denotes an unmutated parent record and does not establish that the reaction was run or succeeded experimentally. `generated_counterfactual` denotes a controlled mutation, never an experimental failure.
- The fixture composition counts, split membership counts, and nine blank human-review rows are artifact checks, not scientific performance. No reviewer judgements, AUROC/AUPRC, calibration, failure-rate, or chemistry-accuracy result has been produced; `metrics_status` remains `not_run`.
- Counterfactual difficulty is assigned by transparent generator design rather than measured human difficulty. Deterministic structural validity means only that the current parser/executor or declared route-order validator accepts the candidate; it is neither chemical plausibility nor feasibility.
- The high-novelty fixture slice uses a declared 0.70 leave-parent-out training-product Morgan threshold. It demonstrates leakage-safe calculation plumbing only and is sensitive to the small authored training reference; it is not a validated scientific cutoff.
- Route counterfactual structural validation covers per-step ReactionIR execution, declared dependency order, and produced/consumed node availability. Phase 10 detects protection timing and fragile-condition mutations through transparent strategy/metadata rules, but these rules are not comprehensive chemical adjudication.
- The research-scale local MVP targets (10,000 recorded reactions, 20,000 counterfactuals, and 5,000 hard structurally valid counterfactuals) remain unrun because no licensed corpus was configured. The 200-record fixture must not be extrapolated to those targets.
- Phase 9 implements model, calibration, uncertainty, abstention, OOD, and ablation contracts but has no licensed research training corpus, adjudicated support-label set, selected model, serialized checkpoint, or reportable performance result. Authored numeric fixtures exercise software paths only.
- Train-standardized maximum absolute z-score is a transparent OOD diagnostic, not a complete density or applicability-domain estimator. Scaffold and reaction-class OOD performance remains unknown until a real grouped study is run.
- Platt and isotonic calibration can fail or overfit on small or shifted calibration sets. Calibration of evidence-support annotations does not turn them into experimental feasibility probabilities.
- Bootstrap intervals capture parent-group resampling variability under the configured data, not chemical uncertainty, hidden corpus bias, label error, or prospective laboratory outcome.
- Forward-reaction evidence remains unavailable by default because no locally reproduced checkpoint with approved license, checksum, preprocessing, and inference contract is configured. The independent critic is disabled by default and can never be the sole plausibility source.
- The Phase 9 in-memory registry does not persist or deserialize estimator artifacts. Research release packaging must add checksum-verified serialization and environment compatibility without implicit downloads.
- Route material continuity can compare explicit IDs and parseable molecular tokens, but complete graph continuity requires mapped expected precursors and stable intermediate records. Missing declarations remain unavailable; local mapping conventions can still prevent cross-step comparison.
- Protection/deprotection and fragile-intermediate checks depend on declared strategy text and metadata tags. They do not cover the full space of protecting groups, reagent compatibility, intermediate isolation, workup, kinetics, or telescoped operations.
- The 0.70 high-novelty route threshold is a transparent descriptive default inherited from the software fixture, not a validated bottleneck threshold. High novelty remains independent of plausibility.
- The eight-case, 40-variant prompt artifact is an authored software fixture selected from the Phase 8 fixture. It has no model outputs or performance result and is too small and synthetic for scientific generalization.
- Prompt “accuracy” is agreement with a declared ReactionIR reference. The reference can itself be incomplete or wrong and is never experimental ground truth. Prompt obedience is not synonymous with chemical correctness.
- No prompt-capable ReactSeq, SynthEx, LLM, or other model was invoked in Phase 10. Provider confidence, calibration, robustness, recovery, and model comparisons remain unmeasured until explicit reproducible research runs are configured.
